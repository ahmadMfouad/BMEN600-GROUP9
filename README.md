## Candidate Project 1

### Biomedical Problem
Automated ECG interpretation software produces a diagnostic statement before a cardiologist reviews the recording, and in many settings that first-pass reading shapes triage and follow-up. ECG characteristics differ systematically between men and women, and diagnostic criteria such as the Sokolow-Lyon voltage criteria were originally developed without distinguishing between sexes. If automated interpretation diverges from cardiologist reading in patterned ways, some patient groups receive a less reliable first read than others.

### Possible Research Question
Where do automated ECG diagnostic statements diverge from cardiologist annotation, and does that divergence concentrate by sex, age, or recording quality? (Compare)

### Dataset
PTB-XL and PTB-XL+ (PhysioNet, open access, CC BY 4.0). 21,799 clinical 12-lead ECGs from 18,869 patients, annotated by up to two cardiologists. PTB-XL+ supplies automatic diagnostic statements from the GE Marquette 12SL algorithm mapped to SNOMED CT for direct comparison against the original human annotation. Patient sex, age, height, weight, and manually annotated signal quality are included in the metadata.
https://physionet.org/content/ptb-xl/1.0.3/
https://physionet.org/content/ptb-xl-plus/1.0.1/

### Biggest Uncertainty
Some PTB-XL records carry an initial autogenerated report rather than a cardiologist-validated one. If the validated share is small, the comparison is partly machine against machine rather than machine against human, and we would need to restrict the analysis to validated records.

## Candidate Project 2

### Biomedical Problem
Falls are the leading cause of injury death among adults over 65. Men and women fall at similar rates, but women report substantially more fall-related injuries, so the disparity lies in the outcome of a fall rather than its frequency. Identifying what separates an injurious fall from a non-injurious one, and whether those factors differ by sex, would help direct prevention efforts more precisely than reducing fall counts alone.

### Possible Research Question
Among older adults who report falling, which factors are associated with whether the fall caused injury, and do those associations differ by sex? (Associate)

### Dataset
Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS), CDC. Public, downloadable, no registration. Respondents are asked how many times they fell in the past 12 months and how many of those falls caused injury, alongside age, sex, general health status, chronic conditions, and physical activity.
https://www.cdc.gov/brfss/

### Biggest Uncertainty
The falls questions are not present in every BRFSS survey year, so we need to confirm a year in which both the falls items and the relevant risk-factor variables were collected. Falls are also self-reported by telephone, and the survey excludes adults living in nursing homes, who are at highest risk.

## Current Decision

Because:
The dataset contains exactly the observations the question requires, and both label sets are already published in comparable form, so no data collection or preprocessing of raw waveforms is needed. The analysis is descriptive and statistical rather than model training, so nothing depends on a model converging.
